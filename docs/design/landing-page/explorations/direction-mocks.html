A. Briefing Paper

A calm, editorial desk where you can see the actual brief before you join.

Borrows from

  • Cursor only: parchment canvas, weight-500 tightened headline, EB Garamond prose, mono metadata, 4px corners, hairlines, framed artifact card, accent colour used as text (pink "tell its story.") and never as a surface

Palette

canvas #F7F5EFink #1C1B17pink #B80F5Ablue #1D3AA6pink tintblue tint

Ink on canvas 15.8:1, pink text 5.9:1, blue text 8.7:1, white on pink button 6.5:1, white on blue button 9.5:1.

Type

Inter Tight 500 headlines (tight tracking), EB Garamond for reading text, IBM Plex Mono only for sources, credentials and labels.

Layout character

Left-aligned, two columns. Text on the left, a real-looking brief card on the right. Hairline rules between sections, no coloured bands. The audience choice is made by two role-coloured buttons.

Trade-offs

  • Pro: answers "what is a brief and can I trust it?" in the first screen. Most credible to journalists.
  • Pro: lowest alarm, calmest tone.
  • Con: least energy. Pink and blue shrink to small tags, so the two-cluster idea is quieter.
  • Con: the hero depends on one good example brief with a real source.

B. Two Voices

The existing two-ink energy, grown up: two sides of one conversation, meeting in the middle.

Borrows from

  • Wise: pill buttons and tags, 28px large cards separated by fill not borders, very heavy tightly tracked display type (softened to sentence case), flat with no gradients
  • Cursor: warm paper canvas, hairline discipline
  • Current identity: overlapping circles, pink/blue split

Palette

paper #FBF8F3ink #14121Apink #D80F6Cblue #1A3BC4pink tint #FFDCEBblue tint #DCE5FF

White on pink 5.0:1, white on blue 8.5:1, pink text on pink tint 5.1:1, blue text on blue tint 6.8:1, ink on tints 14.8:1.

Type

Inter Tight 800 with tight tracking in sentence case (our stand-in for Wise Sans), Inter for everything else, IBM Plex Mono for labels.

Layout character

Centred headline, then the page splits into a pink half and a blue half with the circles meeting at the seam. Each half carries its own audience sentence and its own button. Pill buttons and large rounded panels, flat fills only.

Trade-offs

  • Pro: the strongest expression of "two clusters, one network". Both audiences see their own door in the first screen.
  • Pro: most continuity with what exists, easiest to build on.
  • Con: loudest. Wise's heavy display voice is the one reference quality that can tip into shouting, so I softened it to sentence case. Still a risk with experts.
  • Con: shows no brief in the hero, so the trust story relies on one suggested line.

C. Institute

A quiet, authoritative research institution that happens to have a very good network.

Borrows from

  • Cursor: whisper-weight serif headlines with tight tracking, mono for metadata, sharp 2px corners, flat surfaces with no glow or gradient
  • Wise: the dark-inversion rhythm (a deep field with one light accent on it, then a light section), and the three-column "feature row" for the claims strip

Palette

navy #0A1F66cream #F6F2EApink light #FF9CC6blue light #A9BCFFpink on paper #B80F5A

Cream on navy 13.4:1, pink light on navy 7.7:1, blue light on navy 8.1:1, pink on cream paper 5.8:1. The current pink would fail on navy (2.8:1), so the tint changes on dark.

Type

Fraunces 400 (serif, with one italic phrase) for headlines, Inter for body, IBM Plex Mono for labels and numbering.

Layout character

A full-bleed navy opening with a left-aligned statement and a large flat overlap of two circles. A ruled three-column strip states the positioning. Then a cream reading section. Fewer, larger moves, no cards in the hero.

Trade-offs

  • Pro: most gravity, which suits experts and organisations and the subject. Strongest first impression of seriousness.
  • Pro: the claims strip states vetting, sourcing and reuse in plain sight.
  • Con: coolest in tone, so it may feel institutional to young creators. Blue dominates, so pink and blue are no longer equal partners.
  • Con: dark-field sections need extra care for contrast and a new tint for pink.
