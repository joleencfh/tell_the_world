1. The seam: circles join the two cards

The pair sits on the gap between the audience cards, half on each, so the overlap is literally where the two sides meet.

How it works

The headline now runs the full width and the right column disappears. A pair about 120px wide straddles the top edge of the two cards: pink over the creators card, blue over the experts card, overlap in the gap. On a phone the cards stack and the pair sits in the space between them as a connector.

Trade-offs

  • Pro: smallest footprint of any option, no legend needed, the meaning is built into the layout. Static, so no motion to manage.
  • Pro: the headline gets more room.
  • Con: the pair is small and sits low, so it is a detail, not the first thing you see.
  • Con: it needs the two cards to stay side by side to make sense.

2. Apart, then together: the circles tell the story

The headline says research rarely reaches the people who could tell its story. The circles act that out: apart at the top, overlapping as you scroll, overlapping at the close.

How it works

In the hero the pair starts separated. Over the first screen of scrolling the circles drift together and the overlap appears (using multiply, so the lens is a real mix of pink and blue). The small mark next to the logo does the same, and stays in the sticky header as a quiet progress cue. The closing section shows the finished, overlapped pair. For anyone with reduced motion set, it simply shows the overlapped pair from the start.

Try it: scroll inside the frame below.

Trade-offs

  • Pro: turns the identity into the page's argument, which makes the circles earn their space. Costs almost no layout.
  • Pro: it is the existing scroll-linked idea, just calmer. Uses transforms only.
  • Con: motion, so it needs the reduced-motion fallback and some care on slow phones.
  • Con: the apart state is wider than the overlapped one, so the hero column is slightly larger.

3. The picker: the circles are the role choice

Instead of two cards, the circles are two large buttons. Pick yours and one card appears with your sentence and the waitlist button already set to your role.

How it works

Two labelled discs overlap slightly, so the lens is still visible. Choosing one rings it and fills a single card beneath. That choice becomes the role pre-selected in the waitlist modal, which already exists. Nothing is chosen until the visitor chooses. The prompt text in the empty card is suggested copy. Try it: click a disc below.

Trade-offs

  • Pro: the circles do real work, and the hero is shorter because one card replaces two. Shortens the waitlist form by one step.
  • Pro: clear single action with a clear fork.
  • Con: only one audience's message is visible at a time, so the "both audiences are equal" feel of the side-by-side ledger weakens. A skim reader may see just one.
  • Con: needs proper radio-group behaviour, focus and keyboard handling.
