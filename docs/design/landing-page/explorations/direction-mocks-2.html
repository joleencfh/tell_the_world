Say Briefly and Wise are out. Everything here is derived from the Cursor reference alone. Real copy is used throughout. Decisions so far

  • D1. One action only: join the waitlist. Header "Apply" is removed.
  • D2. Pink = creators & journalists. Blue = researchers & organisations.
  • D3. The Hugging Face / OpenAI incident is real. A source link is needed.
  • D4. No em dashes, no invented proof, WCAG AA throughout.
  • D5. Reference: Cursor only. Say Briefly and Wise are dropped.
  • Proposed. A labelled example brief appears on the page. The "200,000" figure leaves the landing page.

What every direction below keeps from Cursor

  • Warm parchment canvas, warm ink (never pure black or white), Bone cards, hairline borders in warm grey.
  • Weight-400 headlines that tighten as they grow. 4px corners. Flat surfaces with soft warm shadows only on framed artifacts.
  • Accent colour only as text. Here that is the pink and blue audience pair, used the way Cursor uses Ember: short phrases and labels, never button fills or big surfaces.
  • One filled ink button, at most one lighter secondary. Mono for metadata only. Muted greys are darkened to pass AA (Cursor's own Ash, Driftwood and Mist fail).
  • Contrast, measured on parchment: ink 14.3, soft text 6.2, pink 6.0, blue 8.9. On Bone cards: soft 5.9, pink 5.7, blue 8.4. On Linen: pink 5.1, blue 7.5.

D. Product Window

Cursor's own page structure: a plain statement, then the real thing, framed.

Borrows from Cursor

  • Left-aligned weight-400 headline with one accent phrase, ink primary button, framed "window mockup" card with traffic-light dots, centred title and a tab strip, deep warm shadow, 50/50 text-and-artifact sections

Palette

parchment #F7F7F4bone #F2F1EDink #26251Epink text #B80F5Ablue text #1D3AA6

Type

Inter 400 (stand-in for CursorGothic) with progressive tight tracking, EB Garamond for the quoted and editorial text inside the window, IBM Plex Mono for tabs and credentials.

Layout character

Headline and one button, then a full-width window showing a brief with tabs (Brief, Q&A, Quotes, Sources). Experts and creators both appear inside it as labelled voices. The second section is the Quotes list, text left and artifact right.

Trade-offs

  • Pro: the page proves the product instantly. A journalist sees a quote with a name and a source slot in the first screen.
  • Pro: a ready component system (window, tabs, cards) that carries into the app.
  • Con: the two audiences are only labelled inside the window, so the "two doors" idea is quiet.
  • Con: the window may read as a software tool. The content has to feel like reading, not like an IDE, which is why the text is serif.

E. Reading Room

A long, quiet read. The page behaves like the briefs it sells.

Borrows from Cursor

  • The EB Garamond editorial voice, promoted from subheadings to the headline; tight tracking at large sizes; the "literary journal, not SaaS dashboard" feel; hairline rules instead of boxes; accent colour as text only

Palette

paper #F5F3ECink #26251Epink italicblue labelrule #D3CFC2

No cards at all. Only paper, ink and two small accents.

Type

EB Garamond 400 for headlines and reading text at large sizes, Inter only for buttons and nav, IBM Plex Mono for labels.

Layout character

One narrow reading column with big margins. The hero is a typographic statement. Below it a ruled two-column "letter" speaks to each audience in its own colour. The second section is an essay. No product screenshots in the first screens.

Trade-offs

  • Pro: the calmest, most credible and least alarmist tone. Closest to a journalist's own world, and very cheap to make accessible.
  • Pro: the audience split is clear without any box or colour field.
  • Con: shows nothing of the product above the fold, so it relies on the written claim.
  • Con: may feel too quiet for creators. The pink italics are the only energy.

F. Ledger

Two parallel records, side by side: who you are, what you bring, what you get.

Borrows from Cursor

  • Bone product cards with hairline rows, mono metadata labels, the inline diff view from the agent mockups (used for the clarity check), terminal-style field framing, flat structure with one soft shadow on the artifact

Palette

parchmentbone #F2F1EDinkpink labelblue labeladded text tint

Type

Inter 400 headlines with tight tracking, Inter body, IBM Plex Mono doing real work: row labels and the diff itself.

Layout character

Headline left, plain-language subtext right. Below it two matching Bone cards, one per audience, built from labelled rows. The second section shows the clarity check as an inline diff: the jargon struck out, the plain wording added.

Trade-offs

  • Pro: treats both audiences as true equals, with identical structure and one button each. It is the most direct answer to "what do I get?".
  • Pro: the diff makes the clarity check obvious without any explanation, and demonstrates "plain language" visibly.
  • Con: the most "tool-like" and dense. Needs careful wording so it reads as a service, not a spec sheet.
  • Con: the rows need copy that does not exist yet beyond what is shown here.
