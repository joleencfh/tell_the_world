Why this matters now

Attention shows up. Understanding doesn’t stick.

Every few months a warning from inside a frontier lab goes viral and the whole internet spends a week arguing about AI risk. Then it fades, and what’s left is confusion and a fight split down party lines, not a public that understands the problem any better. People are already paying attention. What’s missing is careful explanation from the people who actually study this, coming from voices audiences already trust.

Community Q&A

Ask a question. Get an answer from someone who studies this.

Every brief has open Q&A. Ask what’s actually on your mind, and an expert or organisation working on the problem answers directly, on the record, with their name and affiliation attached.

Why this could be big

Imagine every AI safety expert’s public voice, in one place.

Right now the case for AI safety lives in scattered X threads, Substack posts, and knowledge that never leaves a lab. What if a small, critical group of the world’s communicators could draw from all of it in one place, with a name and a face behind every claim, one question away instead of one search away?

200,000
Creators reached, before journalists even factor in

Just 5% of creators with more than 10,000 followers, across ten major western countries, would bring around 200,000 creators into AI safety communications, each reaching thousands of people directly. Add even a small share of journalists and the reach grows further.
